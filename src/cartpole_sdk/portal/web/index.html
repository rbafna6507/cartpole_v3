A short field guide

Teaching a pendulum to balance.

A moving cart, an unstable pole, and two different ways to stay upright.

Imagine balancing a broom on your palm. You move your hand beneath it to stop it falling. Our cart does the same along a rail. First it must swing a hanging pendulum upward; then it must keep making small corrections to balance it.

The challenge is to do this within a finite rail, with imperfect measurements and a motor that cannot change speed instantly.

01 / Physics-based controller

Rules built from the mechanics.

The controller estimates how much energy the pendulum needs and moves the cart to build its swing. Near upright, it switches to a balance controller that corrects pole angle and angular speed while bringing the cart toward the center.

Gains set how strongly it reacts; the pendulum model helps convert angle error into acceleration. Capture conditions prevent switching to balance too early. Rail recovery brings the cart back when it runs out of room.

The demo uses the proven native auto controller on the Teensy at 1 kHz, with the operator’s prepared gains. The experimental python-auto-trial runs on the Jetson and is saved separately for further calibration and testing.

02 / Learned policy

Behavior learned through practice.

A policy is a small neural network trained in simulation using reinforcement learning. It tries actions, receives rewards for useful behavior, and improves across many simulated episodes. It learns how to move rather than following hand-written swing-up rules.

The demo uses our working.json policy at 100 Hz. It reads cart position and velocity, pole angle and angular velocity, and motion-governor state, then requests an acceleration. Its learned weights determine its decisions; physics-controller gains do not tune the network.

It can discover surprising strategies, including allowing a full rotation before catching upright. Success in simulation helps, but does not guarantee success on the real rig.

What the system measures

The magnetic encoder measures pole rotation. Cart position is estimated from commanded motor steps after homing, so lost steps can make that estimate inaccurate. Filtered signals help estimate velocity, but introduce delay. Calibration establishes where upright and the rail center are.

Vision tracks the pendulum in camera frames. The two angle charts let you compare camera and encoder readings; they are observations, not proof that either sensor is perfectly calibrated. The default demos use encoder feedback, not vision. Tracking and green-mask views are available for the Arducam; the webcam provides an independent view.

From submission to a real run

  1. Choose a demo or upload your work. Submit a Python file, a ZIP with policy.py at its root, or a supported numeric actor JSON. Python implements create_policy(), reset(session), and act(observation), returning an SDK acceleration request or no action.
  2. Review and prepare. Physical runs require operator approval of the exact artifact and a homed, healthy rig. The worker prepares the isolated environment and completes startup before starting the execution lease.
  3. Run within limits. Submitted Python executes in a restricted Linux sandbox without direct device access. The trusted worker checks actions and timing; firmware enforces motion limits, sensor checks, and watchdogs. Native demos use the same queue, supervision, and STOP path.
  4. Inspect and iterate. Run data provides a downloadable ZIP of available observations, actions, events, timing, and a coverage manifest. Detailed recording adds available sensor history and board feedback. Live video is not automatically a saved video recording.

Build your own controller or policy

Use the SDK’s explicit sensor channels and timestamps, test in simulation, replay recorded observations, then submit a bounded hardware run. Keep the observation layout, units, angle convention, and action scaling identical between training and deployment. Training a neural policy requires the supported numeric export; arbitrary pickle and model checkpoint files are not executed.

Use recorded runs to diagnose latency, compare requested actions with measured behavior, and improve your model or training data. Check each bundle’s manifest for missing streams and dropped samples. A good simulation result is a starting point for controlled hardware validation, not a safety certificate.
